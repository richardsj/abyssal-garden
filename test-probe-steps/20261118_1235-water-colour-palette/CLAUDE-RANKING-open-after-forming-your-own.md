# Claude's ranking of the five water palettes (written 2026-11-18, before the owner's)

Judged from four of the nine comparison grids (viewpoint 1, viewpoint 2, flight second 8, flight second 52) at
reduced size. Not judged in motion.

1. **2-same-blue-clearer.** The best balance. Distant rock and the ship's hull show detail that the current water
   hides, coral colours separate better (yellows and pinks survive further), and it is still recognisably the same
   deep-blue scene. Its weakness: shadows are lifted a little, so it has slightly less contrast than the current water.
2. **5-clear-sapphire.** As clear as 2 but with a deeper, more saturated blue in the distance and darker shadows, so
   it keeps the mood and contrast of the current water best. Second only because the complaint was "too thick in its
   blueness", and this one is the bluest of the clear ones.
3. **3-turquoise.** Near things look clean and tropical, but the distance turns a bright, flat cyan and the ship
   loses its silhouette. Closer to a lagoon or a swimming pool than to the sea.
4. **1-current.** The moodiest and the highest contrast, but it hides the middle distance: the "thick" look.
5. **4-pale-aquamarine.** Reads as mist, not as clear water: a milky white haze that flattens everything. Clarity went
   up but so did the brightness of the haze.

If the choice is 2 or 5: a blend is worth trying, 2's numbers with a slightly deeper far colour, to win back the
contrast without the thickness.

A side effect common to all the clearer ones: because light also reaches deeper, the whole picture is brighter. If the
chosen one feels washed out, the fix is exposure or contrast, not going back to thicker water.
